# Chapter 08 — Prioritized research sources and references

For contemporary interaction references, the most useful official sources are the Things guide to Today/Upcoming/Anytime/Someday, which contains illustrative screenshots of each attention-oriented view; the OmniFocus reference manual’s Perspective and Forecast illustrations; Todoist’s illustrated Today/calendar-planning guide; and Sunsama’s timeboxing documentation.  Motion and Reclaim’s official scheduling documentation are particularly useful for studying the opposite end of the interaction spectrum: increasingly automated, constraint-driven placement and rescheduling. 

The strongest synthesis across these sources is not a new productivity methodology but a different **object of optimization**. PIM research says information must return to awareness, interruption research says active cognition must survive suspension, habit research says behavior is context-sensitive, motivational research says control matters, and modern scheduling systems demonstrate that temporal plans can remain flexible. 

The resulting design target is therefore neither a smarter task list nor a more densely optimized calendar. It is a **personal attention-and-resumption system**: a private external cognitive environment that remembers more than the user must remember, exposes less than the user could see, preserves the state of interrupted thought, plans in ranges rather than fantasies of certainty, and progressively learns how one particular person actually lives.
